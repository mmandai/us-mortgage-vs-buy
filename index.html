Rent vs Buy USA

The true cost of buying versus renting in America — closing costs, break-even horizon, price-to-rent ratios by city, and when ownership actually wins.

The comparison most people get wrong

The typical rent vs buy comparison pits a monthly mortgage payment against a monthly rent figure. That misses most of the real cost of buying. Ownership adds closing costs of 2–5% of the purchase price, property taxes, homeowner's insurance, PMI if your down payment is under 20%, HOA fees in many communities, and ongoing maintenance that renters never touch. The monthly mortgage payment is the floor, not the ceiling.

At the same time, renting ignores equity accumulation and offers no protection against rent increases. Neither option wins universally — the outcome depends on how long you stay, local price-to-rent ratios and what you would otherwise do with a down payment.

The gap is significant at today's mortgage rates. It narrows once PMI drops off (typically at 20% equity) and as fixed mortgage payments stay flat while rents rise. The break-even question is how many years it takes for equity accumulation and rent inflation to offset the higher initial cost of owning.

The break-even horizon

Break-even is the point at which cumulative total costs of buying — closing costs, higher monthly outlay, opportunity cost of the down payment — equal cumulative total costs of renting. Below that horizon, renting is cheaper in cash terms. Above it, owning has typically cost less, assuming some home price appreciation.

Market typeApprox break-evenKey driver
High-cost coastal (NYC, SF, LA)10–15+ yearsExtreme price-to-rent ratios

Low-cost interior markets3–5 yearsLow prices, stable rents

These ranges assume ~3% annual home price appreciation and ~3% annual rent growth. At today's rates, break-even has lengthened by 2–3 years compared to the low-rate environment of 2020–2021. Use the Rent vs Buy calculator to model your specific market.

Price-to-rent ratio by major city

The price-to-rent ratio divides a home's purchase price by its annual rent equivalent. A ratio above 25 generally favours renting; below 20 generally favours buying. Most US metros currently sit well above 20, reflecting the impact of pandemic-era price appreciation.

The case for each

Case for buying

  • Fixed mortgage — payment locked for 30 years
  • Equity builds with every payment
  • Home appreciation sheltered from capital gains tax (up to $250k/$500k exclusion)
  • Mortgage interest deduction (if itemizing)
  • No landlord risk — eviction, sale, rent hikes
  • Freedom to renovate and customize

Case for renting

  • Lower upfront capital commitment
  • Geographic flexibility for career moves
  • No maintenance or repair liability
  • Down payment can compound in the market
  • No exposure to falling home prices
  • Easier to right-size as household changes

The opportunity cost of the down payment

A 20% down payment on a $400,000 home is $80,000 of capital that could otherwise remain invested. At a 7% average annual return (long-run S&P 500 real return), $80,000 grows to approximately $309,000 over 20 years. That is the opportunity cost of the down payment — which must be weighed against the equity growth of the home itself.

$80,000 down payment — opportunity cost vs equity, 20 years
Invested in equities @ 7% p.a.~$309,000
Home equity: $400k @ 3% appreciation~$722,000
Less: 30-year mortgage interest paid~$340,000
Less: property tax, insurance, maintenance~$120,000
Less: closing costs~$16,000
Net home wealth gain (approx)~$246,000

On these assumptions, owning comes out ahead — but the margin is narrower than it looks, and the result is highly sensitive to home price growth and mortgage rate. Home sale proceeds on a primary residence are also sheltered from capital gains up to $250,000 (single) or $500,000 (married filing jointly), which the equity investment doesn't enjoy.

How high mortgage rates change the math

The 2020–2021 environment of sub-3% mortgage rates made ownership economics unusually attractive across almost every US market. At 6.5–7%, the monthly cost of buying has risen far faster than rents — pushing break-even timelines out and making the rent-vs-buy decision genuinely market-dependent again.

Rates are unlikely to return to pandemic lows. The common advice to "marry the house, date the rate" — buy now and refinance later — assumes rates will fall meaningfully, which is uncertain. Model multiple rate scenarios before committing.

Verdict

Buying beats renting financially in most US markets if you stay for 6–8 years or more and benefit from normal home price appreciation. At current mortgage rates, renting and investing the difference is genuinely competitive — especially in high price-to-rent coastal metros. The non-financial factors — stability, control, community — often weigh as heavily as the numbers. Run your specific city, timeline and rate assumptions before deciding.

Frequently asked questions

Is it always better to buy than rent in the US?
No — it depends heavily on your local market, how long you plan to stay and current mortgage rates. In high price-to-rent cities like San Francisco or New York, renting and investing the difference can outperform buying over a 10-year horizon. In affordable Midwest markets with lower P/R ratios, buying often wins within 4–5 years.
What closing costs should I expect?
Buyers typically pay 2–5% of the purchase price in closing costs, covering lender origination fees, title insurance, escrow fees, prepaid property taxes and homeowner's insurance, and recording fees. On a $400,000 home that's $8,000–$20,000. Some costs are negotiable or can be rolled into seller concessions in a buyer's market.
What is a good price-to-rent ratio?
As a general rule: below 15 strongly favours buying, 15–20 leans towards buying, 20–25 is neutral to borderline, above 25 favours renting. Most major US metros currently sit between 18 and 30 — with coastal cities at the expensive end and Midwest markets at the affordable end.
Do I pay capital gains tax when I sell my home?
Not typically, up to certain limits. The IRS primary residence exclusion allows single filers to exclude up to $250,000 of gain and married couples filing jointly to exclude up to $500,000, provided you have owned and lived in the home as your primary residence for at least two of the past five years. Gains above these thresholds are taxed at long-term capital gains rates.
Does renting and investing the down payment beat buying?
It can — especially in high price-to-rent markets at today's rates. The key variables are the return on the invested down payment versus home price appreciation, plus the monthly cost difference between renting and owning. A rent vs buy calculator that models both sides over your expected hold period gives a much cleaner answer than rules of thumb.
